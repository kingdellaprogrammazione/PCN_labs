\subsubsection*{Dose array optical inspection}

After development, the dose array was inspected under an optical microscope in both bright-field (BF) and dark-field (DF) illumination. In bright-field mode, underdeveloped areas appear brown, owing to residual PMMA absorbing in that colour range; in dark-field mode, scattered light from the partially defined nano-structures provides higher contrast and better sensitivity to partial resist removal. Table~\ref{tab:dose_array} summarises the observations across the dose sweep from 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ to 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ in steps of 50\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{rp{9.5cm}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Dose ($\mu$C/cm$^2$)} & \textbf{Observation} \\
    \midrule
    400       & Underdeveloped: ring/halo artefact visible under dark field; resist not cleared in the exposed region. \\
    450--550  & Insufficient dose: partial development, PMMA residue still present inside the split-ring features. \\
    600       & Optimal dose: complete development confirmed; proximity correction applied by Beamer is visible as expected dose gradient across the array. \\
    650--800  & Over-dose: proximity backscatter broadens the exposed regions; split-ring gaps partially or fully closed at the highest doses. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Dose array inspection results for the EBL dose sweep (400--800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, step 50\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$). The dose of 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ was identified as the process optimum.}
  \label{tab:dose_array}
\end{table}

The dose of 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ was identified as the process optimum: the split-ring features were fully developed with clean, open gaps, and the proximity correction was evident in the uniformity of feature sizes across regions of differing pattern density. Too low a dose leaves intact PMMA (chain scission incomplete); too high a dose widens the written features through the backscattered dose deposited over a radius of several micrometres around the primary beam footprint.

\subsubsection*{Lift-off and post-lift-off observation}

Following the identification of the optimal dose, 50\,nm of gold was deposited by thermal evaporation and lift-off in acetone was performed on the 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ dose region. At optical microscope inspection after lift-off, the split-ring array at the 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ dose appeared correctly patterned; however, residual PMMA was observed over the sample surface at the microscale, indicating incomplete lift-off in some areas. This residue most likely arises from regions that were only partially sensitised by the electron beam and were therefore not fully dissolved during the 50\,s MIBK:IPA development step; any remaining PMMA acts as a dielectric spacer between the gold nanostructure and the silicon substrate, modifying the local refractive index and thereby altering the plasmonic resonance frequency of the split rings. The lift-off results at multiple EBL doses were subsequently characterised by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) during the third session (Section~\ref{sec:DRIE_mat_met}).
