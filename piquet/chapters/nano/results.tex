\subsubsection*{Dose array optical inspection}

After development, the dose array was inspected under an optical microscope in both bright-field (BF) and dark-field (DF) illumination. In bright-field mode, underdeveloped areas appear brown, owing to residual PMMA absorbing in that colour range; in dark-field mode, scattered light from the partially defined nano-structures provides higher contrast and better sensitivity to partial resist removal. Each written field contains the split-ring resonator array together with a set of auxiliary test structures included in the same layout to assess the achievable resolution: a square array of dot patterns of graded density, numbered from 2 down to 0.1, a radial resolution test pattern in which triangular sectors converge toward a common centre, and a staircase array of decreasing square windows. These auxiliary structures, generated alongside the split-ring layout in the same \textit{Beamer}-corrected \texttt{.CAN} file, provide a continuous, easily recognisable indicator of the local development state and are therefore used throughout this section to compare doses. Representative low-magnification micrographs of the same written field at 400, 600 and 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:nano_dose_overview}, and Table~\ref{tab:dose_array} summarises the observations across the full dose sweep from 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ to 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ in steps of 50\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$.

The complementarity of the two illumination modes is most evident at intermediate doses, where the state of development is not immediately obvious under a single illumination scheme. Figures~\ref{fig:nano_bf} and~\ref{fig:nano_df} compare the same dot-array field at 450\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ under bright-field and dark-field illumination: in bright field, the individual dots are visible only as a faint, low-contrast texture against the surrounding resist; under dark field, the identical dots appear as bright, high-contrast spots, since light scattered by the partially developed sidewalls is collected directly by the objective instead of being reflected away as in the specular bright-field geometry. This confirms that dark-field imaging is the more sensitive tool for judging marginal development at doses close to the threshold, while bright-field imaging remains more informative for the larger, fully-developed features and for the overall colour contrast used to identify residual PMMA.

The qualitative classification in Table~\ref{tab:dose_array} can be complemented by a quantitative method: for each dose step, a consistent geometric proxy is measured on the developed sample: for example, the width of the smallest fully-resolved feature, or the diameter of any residual halo surrounding a radial test pattern; the result is plotted against dose to identify the true optimum as a minimum or inflection point in the measured dimension rather than by visual category alone. This approach removes observer subjectivity from the threshold determination and yields a continuously-varying dose-response curve that can be compared between sessions and resist batches. The radial resolution test pattern included in this group's own layout, in which triangular sectors converge toward a common centre, is precisely the type of structure suited to this analysis: the radius of the region where the individual sectors can no longer be distinguished shrinks as the dose approaches the optimum and grows again once proximity broadening sets in, so that a plot of this radius against dose has a minimum at the optimal dose. Figures~\ref{fig:nano_radial_600} and~\ref{fig:nano_radial_800} show the pattern at 600 and 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$; a systematic measurement of the unresolved radius across the full dose sweep was not carried out in this session, and is left as a natural extension of the present qualitative comparison.

The dose of 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ was identified as the process optimum: the split-ring features were fully developed with clean, open gaps, and the proximity correction was evident in the uniformity of feature sizes across regions of differing pattern density. Too low a dose leaves intact PMMA (chain scission incomplete); too high a dose widens the written features through the backscattered dose deposited over a radius of several micrometres around the primary beam footprint.

the finer dot rows are both crisply defined with uniform linewidth across the
field. At 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ the same structures remain individually resolved,

Table~\ref{tab:dose_array} rather than a fully-closed failure mode; a

\subsubsection*{Lift-off and post-lift-off observation}

Following the identification of the optimal dose, 50\,nm of copper was deposited by thermal evaporation and lift-off in acetone was performed across the dose sweep, from 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ to 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, allowing the same regions inspected before metallisation to be re-examined after pattern transfer. Low-magnification micrographs of the corner alignment marks used for stage navigation between written fields were also collected at each dose but are not reproduced here, as they carry no additional information on pattern quality. At the lowest dose of the sweep, optical inspection after lift-off revealed a diffuse, cloudy haze covering a large fraction of the field, overlaying the numbered dot array, the radial pattern and the alignment structures alike, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:nano_postliftoff_400}. This haze is attributed to PMMA that was only partially sensitised by the electron beam and was therefore not fully dissolved during the 50\,s MIBK:IPA development step: any remaining PMMA acts as a dielectric spacer between the copper nanostructure and the silicon substrate, modifying the local refractive index and thereby altering the plasmonic resonance frequency of the split rings, and here further prevented the acetone bath from fully lifting off the overlying copper, leaving the residue visible under the optical microscope.

  \caption{Optical micrograph of the written field after copper deposition and lift-off at 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$. A diffuse haze of residual, incompletely-developed PMMA covers a large fraction of the field, consistent with incomplete lift-off at this underdosed condition.}
  \label{fig:nano_postliftoff_400}
\end{figure}

Figures~\ref{fig:nano_postliftoff_500} and~\ref{fig:nano_postliftoff_700} show the
intermediate 500 and 700\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ conditions after lift-off. At
500\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, despite the "insufficient dose" classification assigned to
this condition prior to lift-off (Table~\ref{tab:dose_array}), the split-ring
gaps and the graded dot lattice are both already well resolved after
metallisation, with no haze comparable to the 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ case; this
indicates that the pre-lift-off development threshold identified qualitatively
under the optical microscope is conservative with respect to the dose actually
required for successful pattern transfer. At 700\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ the split-ring
linewidth appears visibly thicker than at 500\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, consistent with
the onset of the proximity-broadening regime reported for 650--800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$
in Table~\ref{tab:dose_array}.

At the optimal dose of 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, the split-ring array appeared correctly patterned after lift-off, with individually resolved, open copper rings and no visible haze. Figures~\ref{fig:nano_postliftoff_600} and~\ref{fig:nano_postliftoff_800} compare this region with the equivalent field at 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$: in both frames the split-ring gaps themselves remain open, so that the closing of the gaps anticipated from the pre-lift-off dose array (Table~\ref{tab:dose_array}) is not obviously reproduced in this particular pair of fields. The narrow gap regions of the split-ring geometry are nonetheless the topologically most enclosed areas of the pattern, since the acetone can approach the resist boundary at a gap from only a limited perimeter; this makes these regions the most susceptible to slow lateral undercutting and to the type of localised incomplete lift-off documented above at 400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ (the underlying mechanism is described in the Metallization and Lift-off subsection of the NANO Materials and Methods).

The effect of over-dose is instead more clearly visible in the finest line-and-space category of the auxiliary array, where the calibrated distance tool of the microscope software was used to measure feature dimensions directly on the copper pattern, in the same spirit as the critical-dimension measurements reported for the MICRO experience. Rather than a single pair of fields, four progressively finer categories of the same 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ region were measured this way, shown in Figures~\ref{fig:nano_cd800_r1}--\ref{fig:nano_cd800_merged}: the coarsest category has a line width of 10.78\,$\mu$m and a pitch of 27.6\,$\mu$m; the next has a line width of 8.08\,$\mu$m and a gap of 6.80\,$\mu$m; the next is the cleanly-resolved pair already noted, with a line width of 2.20\,$\mu$m and pitch of 3.66\,$\mu$m; and the finest category measured, with a gap of only 1.87--2.09\,$\mu$m between lines, begins to show the line-edge waviness and, in places, adjacent lines appear to touch, an incipient bridging that is consistent with the proximity backscatter broadening described for this dose range in Table~\ref{tab:dose_array}. This confirms, at the level of the metallised pattern, the same over-dose broadening mechanism identified qualitatively in the PMMA dose array prior to lift-off, and provides a quantitative counterpart to the qualitative dose classification of Table~\ref{tab:dose_array} for the 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ condition specifically; the analogous measurement was not repeated at every dose step in the sweep, so a complete dose-response curve of the kind described above for the radial pattern remains a natural extension of this result.

At the same dose, the finest category of the density-graded dot lattice was
also measured directly after lift-off, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:nano_dotarray_detail}:
the individual copper dots are annular rather than solid disks, with a
measured outer diameter of 3.14\,$\mu$m and an enclosed area of
7.74\,$\mu$m$^2$; a further calibrated distance measurement of 3.04\,$\mu$m,
comparable to the dot diameter itself, was also recorded across several rows of
the lattice. The annular, rim-like cross-section is consistent with an angled or otherwise
non-collimated component in the thermal evaporation flux: copper deposited on
the sloped inner sidewall of the small PMMA opening accumulates preferentially
near the rim, producing a thicker copper ring at the edge of each dot and a
thinner, more easily lifted-off deposit at its centre.
