\textbf{Starting Material and Resist Setup} \\
The fabrication process was performed on a silicon substrate. Polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) 
was utilized as the lithographic mask. PMMA is a high-resolution, positive-tone resist specifically 
optimized for highly energetic Electron Beam Lithography (EBL). The resist was spun onto the silicon 
to a thickness of approximately $300\text{ nm}$. Under electron bombardment, 
the long polymer chains of the PMMA are broken into smaller, soluble molecules, 
allowing for selective removal during the development stage.

\textbf{Design and Proximity Effect Correction} \\
The target structures fabricated were split ring resonators, 
designed to function as nanoscale optical antennas with critical feature gaps of $100\text{ nm}$. 
The initial layout was generated as a CAD file (.gds format) including other structures useful to estimate the 
maximum resolution achievable.
To account for electron scattering and diffusion within the resist (the proximity effect), the design was 
imported into \textit{Beamer} software. The software executed Monte Carlo simulations to calculate and correct the 
localized electron doses, ensuring uniform pattern transfer across areas of varying feature density.

\textbf{EBL Exposure and Dose Calibration} \\
Patterning was executed using an EBL system operating at an acceleration voltage of $50\text{ kV}$. 
Prior to writing, the electron beam current was measured and calibrated to $1.08 \pm 0.01\text{ nA}$ using a Faraday cup. 
To empirically determine the optimal energy threshold for resist clearance, a dose array was patterned. 
The test grid varied the electron dose from $400$ to $800\text{ }\mu\text{C/cm}^2$ in increments of 
$50\text{ }\mu\text{C/cm}^2$. The total execution time is around $35\text{ minutes}$, utilizing a writing speed of $23\text{ mm/s}$.

\textbf{Development} \\
Following exposure, the sample underwent development to remove the fragmented polymer regions. 
The substrate was immersed in a developer solution comprising MIBK (Methyl isobutyl ketone) and Isopropanol in 
a $1:3$ ratio for exactly $50\text{ seconds}$. Immediately after, the sample was rinsed with pure isopropanol to 
halt the chemical development and dried using a steady stream of nitrogen ($N_2$).

\textbf{Metallization and Lift-off} \\
Once the PMMA mask was successfully developed, a $50\text{nm}$ ($0.5\text{ k\AA}$) layer of Gold was deposited over the entire sample. 
The thickness of the deposited metal was precisely monitored using an oscillating crystal thickness monitor. 
Finally, the split ring structures were isolated via a lift-off procedure. 
The sample was submerged in an acetone bath, which dissolved the remaining unexposed PMMA. 
This action "lifted off" the unwanted overlying metal, leaving only the desired gold plated pattern firmly adhered to the silicon substrate.
