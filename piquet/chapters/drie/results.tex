% =========================================================
% DRIE + SEM Imaging -- Results
% =========================================================

In this section the SEM images of the completely treated wafer pieces, processed
according to the procedure described in Section~\ref{sec:DRIE_mat_met}, are
presented and discussed. Two wafer pieces were characterised:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a piece from the MICRO experience wafer (AZ\,5214E-masked silicon,
    trench structures at micrometre scale). A second micro piece patterned
    with a standard positive-tone process could not be fabricated within the
    available session time and was therefore not available for comparison. A future run including a positive-tone sample would provide a direct sidewall profile comparison between the image-reversal undercut geometry shown in Figures~\ref{fig:micro_sem_overview}--\ref{fig:micro_sem_sidewall} and the more nearly-vertical positive-mode profile, which is the relevant reference for etch-and-strip applications where an undercut is not desired.
  \item a piece from the NANO experience wafer (EBL-patterned PMMA on
    silicon, copper split-ring resonators after lift-off).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Microstructure}

At micro scale the Pseudo-Bosch etch was successful and uniform across the chip
surface; the recipe produced consistent, well-defined trench sidewalls, as shown in
Figures~\ref{fig:micro_sem_overview} and \ref{fig:micro_sem_sidewall}. Two
artefacts were nonetheless observed and are documented below.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{piquet/images_DRIE/dopoetching - litopositiva.png}
  \caption{SEM overview of silicon trenches after Pseudo-Bosch DRIE of the
           AZ\,5214E-masked micro sample (positive-tone image-reversal pattern).
           The etch is uniform across the imaged area and the trench geometry
           is well reproduced.}
  \label{fig:micro_sem_overview}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{piquet/images_DRIE/dopoetching - litopositiva_001.png}
  \caption{Higher-magnification SEM view of the etched trench array showing
           sidewall quality. The smooth sidewall profile is consistent with
           the Pseudo-Bosch process, which eliminates the periodic scallops
           produced by the standard alternating Bosch cycle.}
  \label{fig:micro_sem_sidewall}
\end{figure}

Two failure modes were documented. First, \textit{charging effects}: in SEM imaging,
the non-conductive photoresist mask accumulated charge because secondary electrons
generated by the incident beam could not escape through the insulating polymer.
This deflected the imaging beam, producing distorted field lines and reduced image
quality in areas covered by thick resist. Second, \textit{resist adhesion failure}:
as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:micro_resist_failure}, the photoresist locally
detached from the silicon surface during the etch (most likely due to insufficient
HMDS priming or an incomplete soft bake in that region), floated momentarily in
the process environment, and re-adhered randomly on a different part of the
surface. The re-deposited resist fragment acted as an unintended etch mask,
producing an irregular pattern of etched and un-etched areas outside the intended
feature layout.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{piquet/images_DRIE/dopoetching - litopositiva_resist sollevato galleggiato e ridepositato002.png}
  \caption{SEM image of a region where the AZ\,5214E photoresist
           detached from the silicon surface, floated in the etch environment,
           and re-adhered randomly on the sample. The locally unmasked
           silicon areas were etched unintentionally, producing an irregular
           surface topology outside the intended trench pattern.}
  \label{fig:micro_resist_failure}
\end{figure}

The final resist thickness after the $\sim$6\,min etch was measured at
$\sim$700\,nm, compared to the nominal pre-etch thickness of 1.3\,$\mu$m;
approximately 600\,nm of resist was consumed during the process. This consumption is of the expected order of magnitude given the $\sim$1:10 silicon-to-resist selectivity typically reported for photoresist masks in this process (Table~\ref{tab:process_summary}); the corresponding silicon etch depth was not independently recorded for this sample, so this comparison serves as a plausibility check rather than a direct verification of the selectivity figure.

\subsubsection{Nanostructure}

The nano-scale samples were examined in the SEM to characterise the EBL-defined
split-ring resonator structures at different exposure doses.
Figures~\ref{fig:nano_dose600_a}--\ref{fig:nano_dose800} show SEM images of
the copper split-ring resonators on silicon after lift-off at doses ranging from
400\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ to 800\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$, confirming the dose-array observations
made under the optical microscope during the NANO session.

Several phenomena characteristic of nano-scale fabrication were observed in
the SEM images. The measured gap dimension in the split-ring resonators at the
optimal 600\,$\mu$C/cm$^2$ dose was $\sim$58\,nm, against the 100\,nm target
value. Several contributing factors are plausible, though the data collected in this experience do not allow them to be individually disentangled: residual proximity over-exposure not fully corrected by Beamer, lateral under-etch of the PMMA during development broadening the exposed regions, and mask erosion during evaporation. Distinguishing their relative contributions would require additional controls: for example, repeating the dose array with a finer Beamer correction mesh, or measuring gap dimensions before and after evaporation separately to isolate the mask-erosion contribution. \textit{Bowing} (a concave inward curvature of the sidewalls)
was also observed in deeper features; this arises because ion flux in narrow
features is partially deflected sideways by charge accumulation at the feature
bottom, attacking the lower sidewall rather than the floor and increasing the
effective undercut. Finally, some cross-sections showed step profiles at localised positions, indicating non-uniform etch across the chip. Several hypotheses are consistent with this observation (local charging in the copper mask, proximity effects in high-density pattern regions, and gas-flow non-uniformities at nano-scale feature densities), but the available data do not allow their relative contributions to be ranked or separated without dedicated control experiments.
These observations are consistent with the \textit{Aspect Ratio Dependent Etching}
(ARDE) effect described in Section~\ref{sec:DRIE_mat_met}: narrow gaps between
intended pillar bases etch faster in the lateral direction than anticipated under
the macro-scale process model, converting the expected nano-well geometry into
arrays of isolated pillars in some regions.
