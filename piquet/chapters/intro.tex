The Cleanroom Experiences provide an introduction for multiple
sub-experiences in the PiQuET cleanroom.

It is a 500\,m$^2$ cleanroom consisting of multiple cleanrooms
varying in ISO class from ISO~5 to ISO~7.

According to ISO 14644-1 \cite{ISO14644_1}, cleanroom classes are
defined by the maximum allowable concentration of airborne particles
per cubic meter as a function of particle size
(Table~\ref{tab:iso_classes}). And this is maintainable by:
\begin{itemize}
  \item filtration system and environmental control systems
  \item overpressure compared to the external environment
  \item good rules of behaviour
\end{itemize}

In the PiQuET cleanroom the cleanest zone (ISO~5) is used for
photolithography and nanofabrication processes, the ISO~6 corridor
connects the process bays, and the gowning/support area operates at ISO~7.

The Cleanroom Experience consists of three main sub-experiences we
are interested in:
\begin{itemize}
  \item MICRO Experience: photolithography
  \item NANO Experience: Electron Beam Lithography (EBL)
  \item Dry-Etch and SEM Imaging
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Why a Cleanroom?}

All fabrication steps described in this report were performed inside
the PiQuET cleanroom. Particle contamination is the primary threat to
micro- and nano-fabrication: a single dust particle deposited on a
wafer can bridge two conductors, block an etch, or destroy a
nanostructure entirely. To prevent this, the cleanroom maintains a
controlled environment through continuous HEPA/ULPA filtration,
regulated temperature and humidity, positive overpressure relative to
the external environment, and strict behavioural protocols. Every
person entering must wear a full cleanroom suit (hair net, cap, mask,
gloves, overshoes) to minimise particle contamination from biological
sources; the human body is the dominant source of contaminating
particles in a semiconductor processing environment. Defect avoidance
and process reproducibility, the two fundamental requirements of
nano-fabrication, depend critically on operating within a
sufficiently controlled particulate environment; a cleanroom of
appropriate ISO class for the target feature size, such as the
ISO~5--7 zones used here, is the standard means of reliably meeting
these requirements.
