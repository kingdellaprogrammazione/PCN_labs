This part of the experience has the aim of showing the conceptual and physical realization 
of a system able to execute a schematic version of the BB84 protocol, with some caveats. 
Indeed what is shown 
is a simple demonstration where single photons are not produced nor measured, due to the complexity and cost of the 
required setup.
Much bigger light signals are used; nevertheless, the key establishment setup is exactly the same as a simple free-space qkd scheme, with polarizing beam splitters and half-wave plates. The difference  lies in the fact that the detectors employed are designed to detect macroscopic signals 
and are calibrated to simulate the behaviour of single photon detectors, meaning that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item if the light reaching the two receiving detectors is of approximately equal intensity an electronic system will make them emit a random "click", to simulate the random collapse of a single photon's wavefunction on one detector when it is in a superposition of paths;
    \item if the intensity reaching the two receiving detectors is clearly skewed between them the electronic system interpret this as if the photon's wavefunction is completely concentrated into one of the two final paths. The corresponding detector will "click" with certainty.
\end{itemize}
Notice that since the BB84 protocol's security relies on the quantum nature of the physical particles involved, this demonstration miss this property. For example, a photon splitting attack (PNS) (TODO: put the ref) could be carried out easily, tapping the free-space line and extracting a group of photons that make possible to know the final synthethised key by a third party. 

This experience allows to understand so both the theoretical framework and the physical setup needed, giving to the experimenters the possibility to get familiar with the typical issues that can arise on an optical table.
